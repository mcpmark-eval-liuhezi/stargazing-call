### What this does to the sky

- A ~99% Moon adds on the order of a magnitude or more of skyglow. The Milky Way, faint nebulae, and low-surface-brightness galaxies are effectively gone. Bright open clusters, planetary nebulae, and doubles remain workable; bright-planetary and lunar observing are unaffected.
- **Bright-sky targets that remain excellent:** Saturn (opposition was Oct 4, 2026 — still near-peak, up essentially all evening), the nearly-full Moon itself (ray systems around Tycho, maria boundaries), and classic bright doubles (e.g., Albireo, gamma Andromedae).

### Next genuine dark window

- **New Moon: Nov 9, 2026, 2:02 AM EST.** Best dark-sky weekends: **Nov 6–8** (Fri–Sat before new) and **Nov 13–15**. Also note DST ends **Sunday, Nov 1, 2026** — dark by ~6 PM after that, convenient for early setups.

## 5. The decision, reasoned

### Why this is a moonlight call, not a weather call

Both sites sit under the same quiet autumn high-pressure ridge: **clear to mostly clear Friday and Saturday nights, no precipitation, no alerts, light southerly winds Saturday night**. The first cloud of any kind arrives Sunday night (partly cloudy at both), with the next weather system. On sky alone, you could flip a coin between the two.

So the Moon decides it — and the Moon is a hard no for dark-sky work at **either** site:

- Saturday night the Moon is **98.6% illuminated**, rises **5:23 PM** (before the ~6:15–6:30 PM sunset), transits near midnight, and sets **after the ~7:30 AM sunrise**.
- Friday (94.7%) and Sunday (99.8%) are the same story — up all night, every night.
- **There is no dark-sky window this weekend at either site. Period.** A 98% Moon is a 98% Moon in Potter County and on Pendleton County's ridge alike.

### If we go anyway: Cherry Springs, and here's why

1. **Temperature:** Saturday-night low **36 °F vs. 33 °F**, and PA runs ~3 °F warmer every night this week. At 1 AM in late October that's a real comfort/eyepiece-dew difference.
2. **Wind exposure:** The Spruce Knob station is currently reporting **17 mph NW** while Cherry Springs reports **0 mph**. The Knob is an exposed 4,863-ft summit — expect persistent mountaintop breeze and wind chill that the zone forecast understates. Cherry Springs' meadow is sheltered by comparison.
3. **Facilities:** Cherry Springs' **Overnight Astronomy Observation Field is open to registered users all night** (no event conflicts — both 2026 star parties concluded months ago), and the rustic campground is open through late October (this is one of its final weekends). At Spruce Knob, the **lake campground closed for the season Oct 13**; you'd be relying on the summit/lake day-use areas with no overnight facilities.
4. **The Knob's advantage is worthless this weekend:** Spruce Knob earns its reputation through darkness and transparency — exactly what a 98.6% Moon erases. You'd take its colder, windier conditions in exchange for zero dark-sky benefit. Every point of differentiation collapses to logistics, and logistics favor Cherry Springs.

### Night choice, if we go

- **Saturday Oct 24** is the club's primary night and is fine (mostly clear, low 36, S 5 mph).
- **Friday Oct 23 is marginally better** — the PA forecast is **"Clear"** (vs. "mostly clear" Saturday) and the Moon is 94.7% rather than 98.6%. A defensible pick if the group can move a day.
- **Avoid Sunday night** — partly cloudy at both sites as the next system approaches.

### The real play: reschedule the dark-sky trip

The genuinely dark weekends are **Nov 6–8** and **Nov 13–15** (New Moon: **Nov 9, 2026, 2:02 AM EST**). The Milky-Way core season is over for the year anyway; the winter deep-sky show (Orion, the winter clusters and nebulae) is better served by a moonless November night. If the club can only fund one big dark-sky run this fall, spend it there, not under a Hunter's Moon.
